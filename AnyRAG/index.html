Where a RAG pipeline loses its answers

The question

  • RAG searches documents and hands the best passages to an LLM, the reader, which writes the answer.
  • Builders choose a search method, a ranking or selection step, and how much text the reader sees.
  • Which choices matter in Persian and English, and where are answers lost?

The benchmark

  • 156 questions over 155 natural documents: company reports, non-fiction books, statutes
  • Six search pools, three difficulty tiers
  • Half Persian, half English, with annotated gold evidence
  • 90 answerable test questions

What was compared

  • Fixed in advance: four whole pipelines at two evidence budgets
  • Post hoc: retrieval channels, chunk size, query rewriting, embedding models, reranking, LLM selection, five budgets, search agents

The answer

Most wrong answers arise before the reader, not in it: either the complete evidence never reaches the top candidates, or it is dropped when the evidence pack is built.

Post hoc; the test fixed in advance missed its pass mark (see Limits).

Evidence budget
Retrieved characters the reader sees; 6,000 is the tight budget.
Evidence pack
What the reader actually sees, cut to the budget.
Evidence bundle
Passages that together answer a question; a complete pack holds one.
Gold evidence
Passages marked as evidence; used only to score retrieval.
Test fixed in advance
Committed before any test answer was seen; not registered with a third party.
Post hoc
Run after the test answers were known; not confirmatory.

How evidence is annotated

  1. Requirementsthe atomic points a correct answer must meet;
  2. Evidence passagesexact quotes, located by character position in a fixed copy of each document;
  3. Evidence bundlesone or more sets of passages that together suffice.

How the questions were built

  • Drafted by Claude Opus 5.5 agents from a written specification
  • Checked by a mechanical verifier and a separate source-review agent
  • Persian wording reviewed by a native speaker
  • 32 of 156 checked by hand: 31 accepted, 1 fixed

How a RAG pipeline answers

  • Search the pool, order or select candidates, pack them into the budget
  • The pack is all the reader sees
  • All four pipelines share chunks (at most 1,000 characters), prompts and readers

The four pipelines of the test fixed in advance

Pipeline What it does LLM tokens, × Hybrid Median latency
HybridbaselineBM25 and dense (bge-m3) retrieval, merged by reciprocal rank fusion; the budget is filled in rank order.1.001.8 s
HRHybrid + rerankerHybrid, reordered by a cross-encoder reranker: a small trained model that scores question and passage together, with no LLM tokens.1.013.2 s
Lex-RAGour methodFuses BM25, dense and learned-sparse scores. One LLM call splits the question and names a bundle per part; bundles go in first, ranked passages fill the rest.2.04–2.324.0 s
SetR-p+filladapted selectorOur adaptation of SetR: one chain-of-thought call with its published prompt picks a set from Hybrid's candidates, and Hybrid fills the rest. It says nothing about the published system.3.39–3.675.5 s

6,000 characters, all 96 test questions. Tokens: every query-time LLM call. Latency: laptop GPU under contention; only the order is informative.

Readers and scoring

  • Primary reader: GLM-5.3-flash, for every answer and both selection calls.
  • Five readers from other model families answer the same packs.
  • A strict LLM judge: correct only if every required point is met and nothing is contradicted, wrong or unsupported.

Test fixed in advance: not met

  • Pass mark: at least 5 of 90 ahead of both controls at 6,000 characters.
  • Result: 5 to 7 more than Hybrid, 0 to 3 fewer than Hybrid + reranker. Neither primary test was significant.
  • With 90 questions, a few answers is noise. All below is post hoc.

Searching harder

  • An iterative agent (hybrid index, up to five searches, reranker): 77 of 90, the best without annotations.
  • About four times the tokens; median 10.5 s.
  • Its 6 to 8 lead over the reranked pipeline (69 to 71) is within the noise. First judge only.

Reading whole documents

  • Helps only when the right documents are known.
  • Gold documents: 79 correct.
  • Best BM25 documents, cut to the same length: 24.
7

Persian and English

The halves use different documents, so gaps mix language and source. Firm findings come from the cost of a character and from questions asked in the other language.

Token cost

1.5–1.8×

  • Persian costs 1.46 to 1.80× the tokens per character for five of six readers; 2.99× for Qwen3 235B.
  • Budget in tokens: for the primary reader, 6,000 Persian characters ≈ 10,000 English.

Memory gap

20 and 6 of 45

  • No evidence: 20 of 45 English and 6 of 45 Persian correct.
  • Asked in the other language: still 18 and 4 or 5.
  • The gap follows the source texts, not the question language.

Questions in the other language

37 → 2

  • BM25's top 40 holds a complete bundle for only 2 and 8 of 45 (from 37 and 29).
  • Dense retrieval keeps 35 and 28 (from 38 and 33).
  • Reranked pipeline: 53 to 54 correct, down from 69 to 71.

Persian spelling

  • Spelling varies: Arabic or Persian letters, zero-width non-joiners, three digit systems.
  • Unnormalised, BM25 loses 5 to 8 of 45 complete bundles; dense on raw text at most one.
  • Normalise before BM25. Tentative, retrieval only
8

How far to trust this

One benchmark, one primary reader, LLM judges, an author-only partial review, 90 answerable questions.

The test fixed in advance missed its pass mark

  • Pass mark: at least 5 of 90 over both controls at 6,000 characters. Result: 5 to 7 more than Hybrid, 0 to 3 fewer than HR.
  • Neither primary test was significant.

The rest is post hoc

  • All other results reuse the test questions after their answers were known.
  • Their p-values are descriptive; the advice needs a fresh test.

90 questions detect about 11 answers

  • Paired tests have 80% power only for gaps of about 11 answers (about 8 within one language half).
  • A gap of 5 would need about 400 to 460 questions.

Judges and the human review disagree

  • A second judge agrees on 91% of answers (kappa 0.76) but orders the middle pipelines differently.
  • The human review covers 30 questions, blind to the method, by the author alone.
  • Its significant +8 for Lex-RAG over Hybrid fell to +5 (corrected p = .36) with all four pipelines reviewed.

The reader is noisy, about ±2

  • Same packs, same reader again: standard deviation about two answers per cell; single cells moved up to six.
