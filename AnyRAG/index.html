Where RAG loses its answers

The question

  • Which search, ranking and budget choices matter in Persian and English?
More detail
  • RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) finds passages; an LLM, the reader, writes the answer.
  • Search matches keywords (BM25), meaning (dense retrieval), or both.

The benchmark

  • 156 questions on 155 real reports, books and statutes
More detail
  • Six search pools, three difficulty tiers
  • Half Persian, half English, with annotated gold evidence
  • 90 answerable test questions

What was compared

  • Fixed in advance: four whole pipelines at two evidence budgets
More detail
  • Post hoc: search channels, chunk size, rewriting, embeddings, reranking, LLM selection, budgets, agents

The answer

Most wrong answers start before the reader: the evidence is not retrieved, or is cut from the pack.

Post hoc. The test fixed in advance missed its pass mark.

More detail
Evidence budget
Retrieved text the reader sees, in characters; 6,000 is the tight budget.
Evidence pack
The text the reader actually gets, cut to the budget.
Evidence bundle
Passages that together answer a question; a complete pack holds one.
Gold evidence
Passages marked as evidence; used only to score retrieval.
Test fixed in advance
Committed before any test answer was seen; not registered externally.
Post hoc
Run after the test answers were known; not confirmatory.

How evidence is annotated

More detail
  1. Requirementsatomic points a correct answer must meet;
  2. Evidence passagesexact quotes, located by character position;
  3. Evidence bundlessets of passages that together suffice.

How the questions were built

More detail
  • Drafted by Claude Opus 5.5 agents; checked by a verifier and a review agent
  • Two annotators, one a native Persian speaker, reviewed every question together
  • Each question, its answer and its evidence checked and fixed by consensus

How a RAG pipeline answers

  • Search the pool, order or select candidates, pack them into the budget
  • All four pipelines share chunks, prompts and readers

Four pipelines, fixed in advance

More detail
Pipeline What it does LLM tokens, × Hybrid Median latency
HybridbaselineBM25 (keywords) plus dense retrieval (meaning, bge-m3), fused by rank.1.001.8 s
HRHybrid + rerankerHybrid plus a cross-encoder reranker: a small model that rescores each passage. No LLM tokens.1.013.2 s
Lex-RAGour methodFuses BM25, dense and learned-sparse scores. One LLM call picks a bundle per question part; ranked passages fill the rest.2.04–2.324.0 s
SetR-p+filladapted selectorOur SetR adaptation: one chain-of-thought call picks a set from Hybrid's candidates; Hybrid fills the rest. Not the published system.3.39–3.675.5 s

6,000 characters, 96 test questions. Latency on a shared laptop GPU: only the order is reliable.

Readers and scoring

More detail
  • Primary reader: GLM-5.3-flash, also used for both selection calls.
  • Five readers from other model families answer the same packs.
  • A strict LLM judge: every required point met, nothing wrong or unsupported.

  • Result: 5 to 7 more than Hybrid, 0 to 3 fewer than Hybrid + reranker.

More detail
  • Pass mark: at least 5 of 90 above both baselines at 6,000 characters.
More detail

Searching harder

  • Iterative search agent (hybrid index, reranker): 77 of 90, the best without annotations.
  • Up to five searches; about four times the tokens; median 10.5 s.
  • Its 6 to 8 lead over the reranker (69 to 71) is within the noise.

Reading whole documents

  • Helps only when the right documents are known.
  • Gold documents: 79 correct.
  • Top BM25 documents, cut to the same length: 24.
8

Persian and English

Two results do not depend on the documents: token cost and cross-language questions.

Token cost

1.5–1.8×

  • Persian costs 1.46 to 1.80× the tokens per character for five of six readers; Qwen3 235B 2.99×.
More detail
  • Set budgets in tokens: 6,000 Persian characters ≈ 10,000 English (primary reader).

Memory gap

20 and 6 of 45

  • No evidence: 20 of 45 English and 6 of 45 Persian correct.
More detail
  • Asked in the other language: still 18 and 4 or 5.
  • The gap follows the source texts, not the question language.

Questions in the other language

37 → 2

  • BM25 top 40: a complete bundle for only 2 and 8 of 45 (was 37 and 29).
More detail
  • Dense retrieval keeps 35 and 28 (was 38 and 33).
  • Reranked pipeline: 53 to 54 correct, down from 69 to 71.

Persian spelling

  • Normalise before BM25. Tentative, retrieval only
More detail
  • Spelling varies: Arabic or Persian letters, non-joiners, three digit systems.
  • Without normalising, BM25 loses 5 to 8 of 45 complete bundles; dense at most one.
9

How far to trust this

One benchmark, one primary reader, LLM judges, 90 answerable questions.

The rest is post hoc

More detail
  • Other results reuse test questions whose answers were already seen.
  • Their p-values are descriptive; the advice needs a fresh test.

90 questions detect about 11 answers

More detail
  • 80% power only for gaps of about 11 answers (about 8 within one language).
  • Detecting a gap of 5 needs about 400 to 460 questions.

Judges and the human review disagree

More detail
  • A second judge agrees on 91% (kappa 0.76) but reorders the middle pipelines.
  • The human review: 30 questions, blind to the method, by the author alone.
  • Its significant +8 for Lex-RAG over Hybrid fell to +5 (corrected p = .36) with all four reviewed.

The reader is noisy, about ±2

More detail
  • Same packs rerun: standard deviation about 2 answers; single cells moved up to six.
