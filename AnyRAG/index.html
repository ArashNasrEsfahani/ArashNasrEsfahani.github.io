2

Where a RAG pipeline loses its answers

The question

A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system searches a document collection and shows the best passages to a language model, the reader, which writes the answer. Building one means choosing a search method, a way to rank or select passages, and how much text the reader sees. Most comparisons of these choices use English. We asked which choices matter in Persian and in English, and where the answers are lost.

The benchmark

156 questions over 155 natural documents: company reports, non-fiction books and statutes, in six search pools. Questions come in three tiers of difficulty, half in Persian and half in English, and each carries annotated gold evidence. The test set has 90 answerable questions.

What was compared

First, a frozen one-shot test of four whole pipelines at two evidence budgets. Then, post hoc on the same questions: retrieval channels, chunk size, query rewriting, embedding models, reranking and LLM selection, five evidence budgets, and iterative search agents.

The answer

Answers are rarely lost in the reader. They are lost before it: either the complete evidence never reaches the top candidates, or it is dropped when the evidence pack is built.

Evidence budget
The number of characters of retrieved text in the reader's prompt. 6,000 characters is the tight budget of the paper.
Evidence pack
The retrieved text a pipeline actually shows the reader, cut to the budget.
Evidence bundle
A set of passages that together suffice to answer a question. A pack is complete if it holds a whole bundle.
Post hoc
Run on the test questions after their answers were known. Only the frozen test is confirmatory.

How evidence is annotated

  1. Requirementsthe atomic points a correct answer must meet;
  2. Evidence passagesexact quotes with offsets into frozen text;
  3. Evidence bundlesone or more sets of passages that together suffice.

Claude Opus 5.5 agents drafted each question from a written specification; a mechanical verifier and a separate source-review agent checked it; a native Persian speaker reviewed the Persian wording; and 32 of the 156 questions were spot-checked by hand (31 accepted, 1 fixed).

How a RAG pipeline answers

Each pipeline turns a question into an evidence pack, the retrieved text the reader sees. It searches the pool, orders or selects the candidates, and packs them into the budget. All four pipelines share the same chunks (at most 1,000 characters), answer prompts and readers.

The four pipelines of the frozen test

Pipeline What it does LLM tokens, × Hybrid Median latency
HybridbaselineBM25 and dense (bge-m3) retrieval, merged by reciprocal rank fusion. The budget is filled in ranked order.1.001.8 s
HRHybrid + rerankerHybrid, then a cross-encoder reranker rescores the merged list. The reranker is a trained model and uses no LLM tokens.1.013.2 s
Lex-RAGour methodFuses BM25, dense and learned-sparse scores. One LLM call splits the question into parts and names a bundle of passages for each; a packer fits the bundles into the budget and fills the rest.2.04–2.324.0 s
SetR-p+filladapted selectorOne chain-of-thought call with the published SetR prompt picks a set from Hybrid's candidates; Hybrid's list fills the rest. An adaptation, so it says nothing about the published system.3.39–3.675.5 s

At the 6,000-character budget, on all 96 test questions. Tokens count every query-time LLM call. Latency was measured under contention on a laptop GPU, so only the order is informative.

One primary reader, GLM-5.3-flash, answers every question and makes both selection calls. Five readers from other model families answer the identical packs. A strict LLM judge scores each answer: it counts as correct only if it meets every required point and adds no contradiction, wrong value or unsupported claim.

What the results show

Scores are correct answers out of the 90 answerable test questions, for the primary reader and the first judge. A range such as 62–63 covers a few answers the judge could not score.

Searching harder

An iterative agent that searches the hybrid index up to five times answers 77 of 90, the highest score of any arm that does not use the annotations. It needs about four times the tokens and a median 10.5 s. Its lead over the reranked pipeline (69 to 71) is 6 to 8 answers, within the noise of the test.

Reading whole documents

Long context helps only when the right documents are already known. Given the gold documents, the reader answers 79; given the best-matching documents by BM25, cut to the same length, it answers 24.

7

Persian and English

The two halves use different documents, so a gap between them mixes language and source. The firm findings are those a design can isolate: the cost of a character, and every question asked in the other language.

Token cost

1.5–1.8×

A character of Persian evidence costs 1.46 to 1.80 times the tokens of English for five of six readers, and 2.99 times for Qwen3 235B. A 6,000-character Persian pack costs the primary reader about as much as a 10,000-character English one. Set budgets in tokens, not characters.

Memory gap

20 and 6 of 45

With no evidence at all, the reader answers 20 of 45 English questions and 6 of 45 Persian ones. Asked in the other language, English-source questions are still answered from memory 18 times and Persian-source ones 4 or 5 times: the gap follows the source texts, not the language of the question.

Questions in the other language

37 → 2

With every question translated, BM25 finds a complete bundle in its top 40 for 2 and 8 of 45 questions (from 37 and 29); dense retrieval keeps 35 and 28 (from 38 and 33). The reranked pipeline answers 53 to 54 of 90, against 69 to 71.

Persian spelling varies: Arabic or Persian letters, the zero-width non-joiner, and three digit systems. Without normalisation BM25 loses 5 to 8 of 45 complete bundles, while dense retrieval on raw text loses at most one. Normalise before BM25.

8

How far to trust this

Every recommendation on this page rests on one benchmark, one primary reader, one judge and 90 answerable questions. These are the limits, stated plainly.

The frozen gate failed

The only confirmatory test required Lex-RAG to beat both controls by at least 5 of 90 answers at 6,000 characters. It answered 5 to 7 more than Hybrid and 0 to 3 fewer than HR. Neither primary test was significant.

The rest is post hoc

Every other result reuses the same test questions after their answers were known, so its p-values are descriptive. The recommendations need a fresh test.

90 questions detect about 11 answers

A paired comparison has 80% power only for differences of about 11 answers, or about 8 within one language half. A difference of 5 would need about 400 to 460 questions.

Judges and the human review disagree

A second judge agrees with the first on 91% of answers (kappa 0.76), yet orders the middle pipelines differently. A partial human review once gave Lex-RAG a significant lead over Hybrid (+8), which disappeared when all four pipelines were reviewed (+5, corrected p = .36).

The reader is noisy, about ±2

Sending the identical packs to the same reader again gives a standard deviation of about two answers per cell, and single cells moved by up to six.
